== Risk description

=== Related assets

This risk affects the *IoT device* and, through it, the *Distributed control system* (DCS) in the customer's deployment environment.
The device runs inside the customer plant trust boundary and reads process data from the DCS over OPC UA.

The DCS is not part of our product, but it controls a physical process at the customer plant.
Degrading its availability is therefore a production and safety concern for the customer, not only a security concern for us.

=== Threat

The IoT device sends far more OPC UA requests to the customer's DCS than the DCS is provisioned to serve, and the DCS becomes unable to serve its own operators.

No external attacker is needed to cause this: an unreachable or slow DCS endpoint during normal operation is enough to trigger it.
An attacker who can influence the device's network path can also trigger it deliberately, by dropping or delaying responses so that the device keeps retrying.

=== Potential vulnerabilities

The retry logic in the *Runtime data handler* has no backoff and no upper bound on retry attempts.
See the linked security issue, which increases this risk.

=== Consequences

* The DCS becomes slow or unresponsive, and plant operators lose visibility of the process they are controlling.
* The customer stops production as a precaution while the cause is investigated.
* The customer disconnects our device from their network, and the product stops delivering the reporting data it exists to provide.
* The incident is attributed to our product, damaging customer trust and potentially triggering reporting obligations for a device sold in the EU.

== Risk analysis

=== Existing risk mitigations

None at the time of the initial assessment.
The device applies no rate limiting to its outbound OPC UA traffic, and the customer's DCS has no dedicated protection against a misbehaving client on its own network.

=== Justification for initial risk level

Initial likelihood is *moderate*: no attacker is required, and the triggering condition — an unreachable or slow DCS endpoint — occurs in normal operation.

Initial impact is *high*: the affected system controls a physical process, so the realistic outcome is a precautionary production stop rather than a brief service degradation.

This puts the initial risk level above the acceptable risk level defined in the *Risks* register, so the risk must be treated.

== Risk treatment

The chosen strategy is to *mitigate*. A device must not be able to overload a system in its deployment environment, whatever the network does.

=== Risk treatment plan

The security requirement *Limit outbound communications* bounds the device's outbound traffic: bounded retries with exponential backoff, a cap on the request rate per endpoint, and verification that each protocol behaves correctly in error cases and not only on the happy path.

The requirement is verified by test during the Verification phase, under *Test security requirements*.
The error-case tests stay in the release test set rather than being run once at acceptance, because a later change to the retry configuration would reintroduce the risk.

=== Justification for the residual risk

Residual likelihood is *very low*: with bounded retries and a per-endpoint rate cap, the device cannot generate the traffic volume needed to affect the DCS, and the error-case behaviour is covered by tests.

Residual impact is *moderate*: a defect in the rate limiting itself could still raise outbound traffic, but the cap bounds how far it can go before the customer's network monitoring detects it.

The resulting residual risk level is within the acceptable risk level defined in the *Risks* register.

== Risk monitoring

When reviewing this risk, check that:

* the retry and rate limit defaults have not been relaxed in a later release,
* the error-case tests for outbound protocols are still part of the release test set, and
* no security issue reported against the device points to renewed excessive outbound traffic.
